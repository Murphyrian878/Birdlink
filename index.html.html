A feeder that is empty until you ask.

BirdLink gives garden birds one small portion at a time, films every visit, and helps you work out who came. Feeds cost a small fee, and what is left after seed goes to BirdWatch Ireland.

From seed to species

  1. Request a feed

    Pay a small fee on this site. The feeder releases a single portion.

  2. Birds visit

    A motion sensor starts the camera. The tray is cleared again after the feed.

  3. Upload the clip

    Add your recording to the site and an AI suggests which species it saw.

  4. Check and correct

    Fix any wrong ID. Your corrections build a verified Irish garden bird dataset.

Status: BirdLink is a research prototype. Clip identification below works now. Live feed controls and payments are still being built, and the feed button above is a practice demo only.

Identify the birds in your clip

Choose a video or photo from your feeder. The page picks a few frames and asks Claude which species appear. It is a suggestion, not a certainty, so please correct it when it is wrong. Corrections are how we measure accuracy.

Before you start
  • Up to four frames from your clip are sent to Claude, on your own account, to suggest species. The full video stays in your browser.
  • If you choose to share, each answer you give is saved: the clip file name, the suggested and corrected species, the AI's confidence, the time, and an anonymous ID. No video or images are saved.
  • Shared answers are used to measure how accurate AI identification is and to build a verified dataset of Irish garden bird identifications.
  • Please do not put names, addresses or other personal details in file names.

Sharing is optional. If you leave it unticked you still get identifications, and you can download your own answers as a CSV. Final privacy notice and contact details will be added before launch.

Why not just fill a feeder?

Trichomonosis is a fatal finch disease spread through shared food and water. Permanent feeders crowd birds onto the same contaminated surfaces. BirdWatch Ireland now advises taking feeders down from 1 May to 31 October.

No research has tested whether portioned, monitored feeding carries the same risk as permanent feeding. BirdLink is built to find out.

Feeding safety protocol

Every rule below is built into the feeder logic, not left to chance.

  • Rate-limited

    Only one feed can be requested at a time, with a wait before the next.

  • Daylight only

    No feeds are released at night.

  • Daily cap

    The feeder stops once its daily limit is reached, following BirdWatch Ireland guidance.

  • Scheduled cleaning

    The tray and dispenser are cleaned on a fixed schedule.

  • Remote suspension

    Feeding is switched off if disease is reported locally.

What we are testing

TestHow it works
Identification accuracyUsers correct wrong IDs. The correction rate measures accuracy across species and image quality.
EngagementShort surveys before and after using BirdLink, compared with species recorded, uploads and return visits.
Disease monitoringEvery visit is recorded, so sick-bird signs are logged and reported to the Irish Garden Bird Survey's sick finch scheme.
Donation measurementFrequency and value of donations show whether interaction turns into giving.

We expect to learn whether remote interaction increases engagement with local birds, how much a small-fee feeder can raise for charity, how well a general-purpose vision model identifies Irish garden birds, and whether portioned feeding can be run within BirdWatch Ireland's disease guidance.

Where your fee goes

The fee covers seed. The rest goes to BirdWatch Ireland, the charity whose survey data this project relies on.

Who it is for

Schools, nature reserves and community groups can use BirdLink to make local biodiversity visible and to link it to fundraising for bird conservation.
